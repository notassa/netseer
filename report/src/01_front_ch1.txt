@front ABSTRAK
Ancaman siber semakin menyasarkan pejabat kecil, pejabat rumah, perniagaan kecil dan makmal pendidikan, tetapi persekitaran ini sering kekurangan kepakaran keselamatan untuk mentafsir output sistem pengesanan pencerobohan (IDS). Alat IDS sumber terbuka seperti Suricata mampu menjana amaran berstruktur dalam log eve.json, tetapi amaran tersebut bersifat teknikal dan sukar difahami oleh pengguna bukan pakar. Sistem yang dicadangkan, NetSeer, ialah lapisan tafsiran amaran berbantukan AI yang ringan dan berfungsi di atas pengesanan Suricata. Ia menggunakan Pemprosesan Konteks Amaran (ACP), iaitu kaedah pasca pengesanan berbilang peringkat yang deterministik untuk menormalkan, memperkaya, mengagregat, mengaitkan dan mengutamakan amaran Suricata sebelum membina pakej bukti berstruktur bagi tafsiran berbantukan AI melalui Penjanaan Diperkaya Dapatan Semula (RAG). Output model bahasa disahkan semula terhadap pakej bukti untuk mengesan dakwaan yang tidak disokong. NetSeer menganalisis fail tangkapan paket yang dimuat naik dan juga trafik langsung daripada port cermin suis, dan keputusan dipaparkan melalui papan pemuka berasaskan web untuk penggunaan setempat pada perkakasan sederhana. Risiko setiap insiden dinilai menggunakan faktor kebarangkalian dan impak yang diterbitkan secara automatik daripada bukti, manakala risiko peranti dan kesihatan rangkaian dikira secara kebarangkalian. Sistem ini tidak menggantikan enjin pengesanan, tidak meningkatkan ketepatan pengesanan dan bukan platform Pengurusan Maklumat dan Peristiwa Keselamatan (SIEM) yang lengkap; sebaliknya ia mengubah bukti amaran Suricata mentah kepada penjelasan yang kontekstual, diutamakan mengikut risiko dan berasaskan bukti. Prototaip yang merangkumi pemerolehan data, ACP, papan pemuka web dan modul tafsiran LLM setempat asas telah dibangunkan menggunakan model Pembangunan Aplikasi Pantas (RAD). Penilaian merangkumi pengesahan amaran Suricata terhadap kebenaran asas UNSW-NB15, pengurangan jumlah amaran oleh ACP, kesetiaan dan kebergunaan tafsiran berbantukan AI, serta kefahaman pengguna.

@front ABSTRACT
Cyber threats increasingly target small offices, home offices, small businesses and educational laboratories, but these environments often lack the security expertise needed to interpret the output of intrusion detection systems (IDS). Open-source IDS tools such as Suricata generate structured alerts in the eve.json log, but such alerts are technical and hard to understand for non-expert users. The proposed system, NetSeer, is a lightweight AI-assisted alert interpretation layer that works on top of Suricata detection. It uses Alert-Context Processing (ACP), a deterministic, multi-stage post-detection method that normalises, enriches, aggregates, correlates and prioritises Suricata alerts before building a structured evidence package for AI-assisted interpretation with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). The language-model output is verified against the evidence package to flag unsupported claims. NetSeer analyses both uploaded packet captures and live traffic from a switch mirror port, and presents results through a web-based dashboard designed for local deployment on modest hardware. Incident risk is rated with likelihood and impact factors derived automatically from the evidence, and device risk and network health are aggregated probabilistically. The proposed system does not replace the detection engine, does not improve detection accuracy and is not a full Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) platform; instead, it transforms raw Suricata alert evidence into contextualised, risk-prioritised and evidence-grounded explanations. A prototype covering data acquisition, ACP, the web dashboard and a baseline local-LLM interpretation module has been developed following the Rapid Application Development (RAD) model. Evaluation of the system includes Suricata alert validation against the UNSW-NB15 ground truth, reduction of alert volume by ACP, faithfulness and usefulness of the AI-assisted interpretation, and user comprehension.

@chapter CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION
@h2 1.1 Introduction
This chapter introduces the proposed system, a lightweight alert interpretation system for intrusion detection monitoring in the Oriental Tech network environment. It presents the background of the study, the problem statement, the aim and objectives, the scope, the significance of the study and the organisation of the report. Small networks, including small offices and home offices, small businesses and educational laboratories, increasingly rely on networked services while frequently being short of resources and security expertise. Open-source intrusion detection systems are cost-effective, but the alerts they produce remain too technical for users without security training. The proposed system addresses this problem by interpreting security alerts for such users.
@h2 1.2 Background of the Study
Home offices, small businesses and educational laboratories are increasingly targeted by cybercriminals and are considered small networks (Chidukwani et al., 2022; Tetteh, 2024). These organisations are attractive targets because they hold valuable data while typically lacking the staff, budget and expertise of larger enterprises (Manzoor et al., 2024). To monitor malicious activity affordably, open-source intrusion detection systems (IDS) such as Suricata and Snort have become a practical alternative to commercial solutions. However, deploying an IDS is only part of the challenge. Intrusion detection tools require technical knowledge to interpret their output, which not every small-network user possesses. Suricata produces structured alert records in its eve.json log, but a non-expert administrator may see a signature name, an IP address and a severity value without understanding whether the alert represents reconnaissance, an exploit attempt, a false alarm or a genuine incident. Security alerts also often fail to explain why they were raised or the context in which they occurred (Bhusal & Rastogi, 2022).
The problem is compounded by alert volume and false positives. Network intrusion detection systems (NIDS) produce a large number of low-quality alerts, a significant portion of which are false positives, resulting in alert fatigue (Albasheer et al., 2022). For small-network users without analyst training, this creates a gap between detection and understanding: the system may successfully generate alerts, but the user may still be unable to understand or act upon them. This gap between detection and understanding, rather than detection accuracy itself, is the focus of this project.
@h2 1.3 Problem Statement
Oriental Tech faces difficulties in understanding its IDS alerts. While low-cost intrusion detection is now available for small networks, interpreting intrusion detection alerts remains a challenge for non-expert users. Suricata and similar tools generate technically detailed alerts that suit trained analysts rather than home-office, small-business or educational-laboratory administrators. As a result, users may not know which alerts to act on, how related alerts connect, or what action is appropriate. There are three main reasons for this problem.
First, raw alerts are presented in isolation and without context, which makes them hard to understand; combined with a high volume of alerts and false alarms, this leads to alert fatigue and missed alerts (Bhusal & Rastogi, 2022; Albasheer et al., 2022). Second, although dashboards and Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) platforms exist, small-network administrators may find them difficult to use because they require configuration and a certain level of expertise to operate (Manzoor et al., 2024). Third, AI language models can produce fluent explanations, but without structured evidence they may produce explanations that are incorrect or hallucinated (Alhuzali, 2025).
Therefore, a lightweight solution is needed that creates structured, contextualised alert content and presents understandable, evidence-based explanations to non-expert users, without replacing the detection engine or attempting to be a complete SIEM.
@h2 1.4 Aim
This project aims to design, develop and evaluate a lightweight, AI-assisted, explainable alert interpretation system that improves the understanding and usability of intrusion detection outputs for non-expert users in Oriental Tech's network environment.
@h2 1.5 Objectives
The following objectives are set out to achieve the aim:
@list
i. To investigate the difficulties of interpreting intrusion detection alerts and the post-detection processing methods relevant to small-network contexts.
ii. To develop a web-based system that applies Alert-Context Processing (ACP), Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and LLM-assisted interpretation in order to present Suricata alerts as understandable explanations for users.
iii. To evaluate the proposed system in terms of Suricata alert validation against dataset ground truth, alert volume reduction through ACP, faithfulness and usefulness of the AI-assisted interpretation, and user comprehension.
@endlist
@h2 1.6 Scope of the Study
The scope of this study is defined as follows:
@bullets
- The study is limited to the design, development and evaluation of an alert interpretation system for intrusion detection monitoring in small network environments.
- The target users are non-expert administrators of the Oriental Tech office.
- The proposed system interprets alerts generated by signature-based intrusion detection systems such as Suricata; it does not modify or improve the detection engine itself.
- The interpretation is delivered as a web-based dashboard intended for local deployment on modest hardware. Two analysis modes are supported through the same processing pipeline: offline analysis of uploaded packet-capture (PCAP) files, and real-time monitoring of traffic copied from a switch mirror (SPAN) port.
- The evaluation uses public PCAP data, including UNSW-NB15 captures where applicable, together with controlled or simulated traffic. Dataset labels are matched with Suricata alerts using timestamp, addresses, ports, protocol and attack category.
- External enrichment services (AbuseIPDB, VirusTotal) and a cloud language-model API are optional, disabled by default and enabled only with the user's own API key; the default configuration runs fully on-premises with a locally hosted model.
- The proposed system supports baseline security monitoring for small organisations, in line with the spirit of Malaysia's National Cyber Security Policy (NCSP) and the Cyber Security Act 2024 (Act 854).
- The study does not replace or replicate enterprise SIEM capabilities; it addresses the specific needs of lightweight, explainable security monitoring for resource-constrained settings.
@endbullets
@h2 1.7 Significance of the Study
This project addresses Oriental Tech's difficulties in understanding IDS alerts within its network. It also contributes to the field of cybersecurity by addressing the often-overlooked gap between detection and understanding in small network environments. Its significance can be summarised as follows:
@bullets
- By transforming raw IDS alerts into contextualised, human-readable information, the proposed system enables non-expert users to make informed security decisions without requiring deep technical knowledge.
- Context-aware correlation and risk-based prioritisation help users focus on high-priority events, reducing the desensitisation caused by constant low-severity notifications.
- The lightweight design allows deployment on modest hardware without ongoing cloud subscription costs, making security monitoring financially and operationally feasible for small organisations.
- While extensive literature exists on IDS detection accuracy, few studies have systematically addressed the interpretability challenge for non-expert small-network users. This study provides a practical, evaluated approach that can inform future lightweight security tools.
- In its default configuration all processing, including language-model inference, stays on-premises, which protects the privacy of sensitive network data. Optional external services are used only when the user explicitly enables them.
@endbullets
@h2 1.8 Organisation of the Report
This dissertation is organised into six chapters. Chapter 1 presents the background, problem statement, aim, objectives, scope and significance of the proposed system. Chapter 2 reviews previous studies related to intrusion detection systems, Suricata alert output, alert post-processing methods (normalisation, enrichment, aggregation, correlation and risk-based prioritisation) leading to Alert-Context Processing, AI-assisted alert interpretation and Retrieval-Augmented Generation, web-based alert analysis systems and datasets, and it identifies the research gap that motivates the proposed system. Chapter 3 describes the methodology, based on the Rapid Application Development (RAD) model, including the project phases, the requirements, the evaluation plan, the system requirements and the project planning. Chapter 4 presents the design and development of the proposed system, including the ACP pipeline, the AI-assisted interpretation module, the web dashboard and the development progress of the prototype. Chapter 5 reports the testing and evaluation results, including alert validation, alert volume reduction, interpretation quality and user comprehension. Chapter 6 summarises the findings, reflects on the achievement of the objectives, discusses limitations and proposes directions for future work.
